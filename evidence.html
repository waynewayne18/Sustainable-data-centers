Land suitability screening · Great Britain

Common Ground

Britain pays wind farms to switch off because the network cannot carry their output south. This model scores every square kilometre of the country against the constraints that ought to govern where new data centres go — farmland quality, flood risk, protected landscapes, water stress — and puts that on the same map as the wasted power.

The finding

The wasted power and the constrained land are in different countries

Wind curtailment is not estimated here. It is read directly from the volumes the system operator publishes: every bid accepted on a wind balancing unit is that farm being paid to stop generating. Across 2025/26 that is 277,345 records, matched to physical locations through the Renewable Energy Planning Database.

96.6%
of measured wind curtailment sits north of the main transmission constraint, at British National Grid northing 600 000 m — roughly the Anglo‑Scottish border. 97.9% of the national curtailed volume was successfully placed to a location. The figure is a share rather than a total, so it is unaffected by any scaling error in the aggregation.
Method

Three ways a constraint can act

Every layer is a raster aligned to the same 1 km grid, so a national query is array arithmetic rather than a spatial join. Each constraint acts in exactly one of three ways, and never in two — a layer that both scores and filters does neither job properly.

Peat was checked and deliberately left out

Natural England’s Peaty Soils Location dataset is published under the Non‑Commercial Government Licence. The open‑licensed alternative, the England Peat Map, published in May 2025 at 10 m resolution and has not been ingested. Peat is the next layer, not an oversight.

Limitations

What this model cannot tell you

1 km is regional screening, not site selection

The model can say a district merits investigation. It cannot say build here. A real facility footprint is far smaller than a cell, and everything that decides a specific parcel — ownership, ground conditions, local objection — is outside it.

The siting layers are England‑only

Agricultural land grades, flood zones and the water stress determination exist for England. Scotland and Wales have equivalents from different bodies that are not interchangeable, so the map covers England. That gap is itself the finding: the places with the trapped power are the places that cannot yet be screened on comparable terms.

Proximity to curtailment is not access to it

A site next to a curtailed wind farm draws through the same constrained network as anyone else. Absorbing that power requires co‑location, a private wire, or local network headroom. The model finds where surplus generation is trapped; the connection arrangement is a separate question.

Water stress is a metering determination

The Environment Agency states that its classification exists to decide whether compulsory metering may be considered, and must not be used for development planning. It is used here as what it is: the areas government has formally determined to be under serious water stress. That is a governance point, not a hydrological one.

The curtailment total is not quoted

The share north of the constraint is a ratio and survives any scaling error. The absolute annual volume has not been calibrated against published figures and is not presented.

The map has a shelf life

Transmission upgrades now under construction are designed to relieve exactly this constraint. The model quantifies how much of the case disappears when they complete.

Common Ground

Great Britain excludes Northern Ireland, which sits on the Irish Grid with separate agencies. Excluded deliberately rather than silently.

Scoring is deterministic arithmetic over aligned rasters, and is not described as AI. Machine reading of planning applications, and a revealed‑preference model trained on existing facility locations, are the next stages and are not built.
